# RIGHT OR WRONG — Narrative Bible (v0.3 design)

## The premise

**Genre:** Open-world narrative action RPG with six playable sports disciplines, institutional investigation, and branching careers.

**Logline:** In a wealthy but deeply unequal city, an 18-year-old athlete with Olympic-level potential is recruited into a new state-backed, privately financed armed response squad. Under a decorated general, a cohort of exceptional young athletes enters a system divided between civic duty, private influence, and criminal power. Exceptional training gives them choices—but no one can train them to make the right one.

**Core question:** *What do you owe a city that has given you talent and opportunity, but never offered everyone the same chances?*

**Player promise:** The player is not permanently assigned a good or evil class. Sports, public service, complicity, resistance, and criminal ambition overlap. Consequences emerge from particular decisions, not a one-dimensional morality meter.

## Working setting — GREYHAVEN (fictional; rename freely)

Greyhaven is a dense coastal metropolis built around an old industrial waterfront. The city has genuine prosperity and genuine hardship. Some officials serve residents honorably; some are compromised, intimidated or corrupt. The crime world is not one monolithic gang, and poor neighborhoods must not be portrayed as inherently criminal.

- **Highcrest:** towers, private clubs, donor events, corporate boardrooms, and political influence. Wealth is visible; accountability is hard to find.
- **South Ward:** schools, small businesses, families, public housing, clinics, and community sports facilities. Social solidarity is as visible as shortages and risk.
- **Civic Center:** city council, courts, police headquarters, and the Vanguard Academy.
- **Docklands:** warehouses, freight yards, labor unions, and rival groups pursuing legitimate or illegitimate business.
- **The Games Quarter:** Olympic-style training venues open to recruits and civilian athletes.

The contrasts should be observable through infrastructure, public services, NPC routines, storefronts, and mission outcomes—not just exposition or criminal encounters.

## The player

- **Age:** 18 when the story begins.
- **Presentation:** player selects male or female as specified. This is a character-creation choice; future character creator will also include faces, clothing, voice, body build, identity, and name. Prototype v0.2 currently stores the profile choice but uses the same placeholder model.
- **Background:** born and raised in Greyhaven. Already an exceptional athlete who has passed entry assessment for national-level/Olympic-track training; not yet an Olympic champion by default.
- **Motivation:** a shot at a bigger life, pride in training, a desire to protect people, and personal ambition. Players shape the balance.
- **Starting situation:** recruited into **Vanguard**, a newly authorized state program with a mixed public/private funding model, controversial paramilitary equipment, and unclear civilian oversight.

## Vanguard — the recruitment unit

**Working name:** Vanguard Response Directorate (VRD).

The state officially authorizes and funds the directorate. A powerful investor supplies additional money, facilities, equipment, and political leverage through a disputed public-private agreement. Vanguard's stated mission is public protection and high-risk emergency response. Its practical use becomes contested: is it rescuing the vulnerable, guarding property, building political power, or all three?

**Leader:** General Adrian Ward — distinguished retired military commander and skilled organizer, convinced disciplined people can save the city. He has a real ethical code but a blind spot about the compromises he accepts to keep his unit alive.

**Benefactor:** Elias Vale — extraordinarily wealthy infrastructure investor, public philanthropist, major funder of Vanguard. He is persuasive rather than cartoonishly evil. His contracts and private interests deserve scrutiny.

**Civilian oversight figure:** Dr. Mara Chen — independent inspector responsible for reviewing state contracts and use of force. She may be ally, critic, or obstacle depending on player behavior.

**Community figure:** Imani Brooks — South Ward coach and youth organizer, respected by families whose children were overlooked by elite programs.

**Criminal intermediary:** Nico Serrano — broker between illicit fight circuits, corrupt suppliers, and political fixers, aware of secrets he can trade.

**Rival recruit:** Jordan Cross — outstanding competitor who believes obedience to the squad outweighs personal doubts; can become friend, competitor, or adversary.

All names and city details are **creative placeholders**, not user-confirmed canon.

## Why Olympic-caliber athletes?

The recruitment effort selects candidates distinguished by discipline, composure, endurance, tactical awareness, and rapid learning. Recruits come from the six featured sports: Karate, Judo, Taekwondo, Archery, Fencing, and Olympic-style Sport Shooting. Their sport techniques and competition pathways remain distinct. Tactical equipment does not magically transform sport experience into military competence: Vanguard provides additional supervised, fictionalized unit training.

## Story structure

### ACT I — THE INVITATION

Opening scene: dawn. The player runs past two sides of the same city: shuttered clinics and gleaming riverfront towers. Broadcasts celebrate Vanguard's launch while residents argue about who it protects. At induction, General Ward promises service and opportunity. Vale promises resources; a South Ward coach warns that funding always comes with a price.

The player trains in their primary sport, takes the first official assignment, and discovers evidence connecting public funding, a private contractor, and a local criminal intermediary. A seemingly small choice determines which faction trusts them.

### ACT II — THE SPLIT CITY

Each sport becomes a real progression track. Tournaments bring celebrity and sponsors, while street-level missions expose the damage done by corruption. Police, inspectors, city officials, competitors, residents, and criminal groups act according to their own interests. The player can disclose evidence, suppress it, manipulate it, or abandon the system.

A friend in Vanguard may defend an unjust order; a publicly unpopular official may be protecting witnesses; an apparently generous donor may actually be playing multiple sides.

### ACT III — THE PRICE OF POWER

Citywide crisis: a championship weekend coincides with a political funding vote and an organized attempt to control key evidence. The player's earlier choices change who arrives to help, what is believed, and what legal and personal consequences follow.

Endings should reflect **evidence preserved/destroyed, people protected/harmed, obligations honored/broken, career choices, and public accountability**. Avoid a simplistic 'law always right / crime always wrong' score system.

## First playable chapter (v0.2)

1. **The Recruit** — choose male or female recruit profile; the player is 18.
2. **The General** — travel to Vanguard Academy, press `E`, receive General Ward's induction.
3. **The City Is Watching** — explore Highcrest and South Ward, meet locations, enter a basic karate challenge or the underground club.
4. **The First File** — accept police assignment; find investigation clue. The file concerns a contractor with ties to the new unit.
5. **A Decision, Not a Label** — choose:
   - `1` File through Vanguard command: stronger law/benefactor relationship, but reduced public trust.
   - `2` Give it to independent oversight: greater public trust, less investor favor.
   - `3` Sell it to a fixer: immediate money/criminal reputation at the cost of public trust.
6. **Consequences (future)** — missions and NPC reactions branch from the recorded choice. In v0.2 only values and save state change; future chapters are not implemented.

## Ground rules for narrative tone

- The 18-year-old protagonist is not assumed to have mastered lethal tactics or been sworn into full police authority. Early Vanguard membership is probationary and supervised.
- Include credible journalists, public defenders, honest officials, independent inspectors, community leaders, criminal organizers, and ordinary athletes.
- The player can change course; reform, accountability, and rehabilitation are valid narrative paths.
- Distinguish the competition sport of target shooting from any later fictional tactical firearms gameplay.
- Sports competitions need judges, clear rules, training routines, sanctioned venues, and consequences for fouls.
- No real government, police department, city, or Olympic organization is alleged to be corrupt by this fictional setting.

## First playable Academy milestone (v0.3)

The new Vanguard opening progression is now: create 18-year-old recruit → induction with General Ward → select a primary sport from six → interact with fellow recruit Jordan Cross and coach Imani Brooks → complete a sport-specific, three-cue Academy **placeholder** training session → unlock first field assignment → select evidence route → visit the corresponding follow-up location and decide whether to double down or change direction. Story markers and small dialog strings stand in for full cutscenes and character animation.

The creative priority going forward is not a simplistic morality bar. Ward, Vale, Brooks, Cross, Nico, and the oversight office have understandable yet conflicting motives, and the city has both trustworthy and compromised figures across institutions and income levels.
